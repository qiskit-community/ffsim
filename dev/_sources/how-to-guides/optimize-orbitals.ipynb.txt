   "source": [
    "For both real and complex optimization, the returned rotation is $U = \\exp(K)U_0$, where $U_0$ is the initial rotation and `result.x` parameterizes the additional rotation $\\exp(K)$. The initial rotation stays fixed throughout optimization, and the generator parameters start at zero. This keeps all rotation directions available at initialization, including when $U_0$ has several eigenvalues equal to $-1$. Bounds, constraints, and callbacks use these incremental generator parameters. For example, in two real orbitals, an initial rotation of $0.1$ radians and an additional rotation of $0.2$ radians give a total rotation of $0.3$ radians."
   ]

    "## Real vs. complex orbital rotations\n",
    "\n",
    "For $N$ spatial orbitals, the `real` keyword argument controls whether the optimization uses real orthogonal rotations or general complex unitary rotations:\n",
    "\n",
    "- When `real=True`, the rotation is parameterized by $N(N - 1) / 2$ skew-symmetric generator parameters and stays in the determinant component of the initial rotation. The initial rotation defaults to identity (determinant $+1$); an initial rotation with determinant $-1$ is also honored.\n",
    "- When `real=False`, the rotation is parameterized by $N^2$ anti-Hermitian generator parameters.\n",
    "- When `real=None` (the default), `ffsim.optimize_orbitals` automatically detects whether the RDMs, Hamiltonian, and initial orbital rotation are all real-valued, and defaults to real optimization if so.\n",
    "\n",
    "With one orbital, real optimization raises a `ValueError` because there are no continuous parameters. Complex optimization (`real=False`) is supported; its single phase parameter does not change the energy, but SciPy can still enforce constraints on it.\n",
    "\n",
    "If we initialize with a complex orbital rotation (or pass `real=False`), the optimizer optimizes over the full unitary group:"

    "\n",
    "To express the optimized orbitals in the atomic orbital basis, apply the orbital rotation matrix $U$ to the active-space molecular orbital coefficient matrix $C$:\n",
    "\n",
    "$$\n",
    "C_{\\text{opt}} = C_{\\text{active}} U\n",
    "$$\n",
    "\n",
    "where $C_{\\text{active}}$ contains the active-space columns of the original Hartree-Fock MO coefficients."
